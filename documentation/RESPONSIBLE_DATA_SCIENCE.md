# Responsible Data Science

## Overview

The Cost of Aging examines medication costs and insurance coverage using synthetic healthcare records. Because the project involves healthcare-related information and financial measures, its findings must be interpreted carefully.

This document outlines the project's approach to responsible data use, analytical limitations, and interpretation of results.

## 1. Use of Synthetic Healthcare Data

The project uses synthetic data generated by Synthea rather than actual patient medical records.

Synthetic data allows the project to demonstrate healthcare data engineering and analytical techniques without directly exposing real patient information.

However, synthetic data does not necessarily reflect the full complexity, diversity, or financial experiences of real patients and healthcare systems.

The results should therefore be understood as findings within the provided synthetic dataset, not as estimates of real-world healthcare costs.

## 2. Privacy and Data Protection

The project does not intentionally use actual patient records or personally identifiable information from real individuals.

Although the source data is synthetic, healthcare datasets can contain sensitive types of information. The project focuses its analysis on aggregated results rather than individual-level patient information.

## 3. Financial Interpretation

An important distinction in this project is the difference between medication costs not covered by an insurance payer and actual patient out-of-pocket spending.

The pipeline calculates:

```python
calculated_uncovered_cost = total_cost - payer_coverage
```

This value represents the portion of the recorded medication cost not covered by the payer in the source data.

It should not automatically be interpreted as the amount paid by a patient. Other factors, including secondary insurance, assistance programs, contractual adjustments, and other payment arrangements, may affect the amount a patient actually owes.

Similarly, the project's `coverage_proportion` measures the proportion of recorded medication costs covered by the payer. It does not measure the entirety of a patient's healthcare coverage or financial burden.

## 4. Age Group Comparisons

Patient age is calculated at the time a medication record begins, rather than using a patient's present-day age.

Records are grouped into defined age categories to support comparisons across different stages of life.

These categories are analytical groupings and do not necessarily represent meaningful clinical or insurance eligibility boundaries.

Comparisons across age groups should also account for differences in the number of records, medication utilization, and payer representation.

## 5. Limitations and Potential Bias

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the project's findings:

- **Synthetic data:** The dataset is generated rather than collected from actual patient experiences.
- **Representativeness:** The records may not reflect the demographic, geographic, or socioeconomic diversity of the broader population.
- **Coverage interpretation:** Payer coverage in the dataset does not necessarily represent a patient's final financial responsibility.
- **Medication records:** The number of medication records is not equivalent to the number of unique medications or unique patients.
- **Aggregation:** Grouping records by age and payer can conceal differences among individual patients and medication types.
- **Association versus causation:** Observed differences across age groups do not establish that aging causes changes in medication costs or insurance coverage.

These limitations should be considered when presenting conclusions or making comparisons.

## 6. Responsible Communication of Findings

The project aims to communicate patterns in medication costs and payer coverage without overstating what the data can establish.

Visualizations and conclusions should:

- Clearly identify the data as synthetic.
- Distinguish payer coverage from actual patient out-of-pocket expenses.
- Provide appropriate context for comparisons across age groups and payers.
- Avoid implying that observed patterns apply universally to real patients.
- Avoid causal claims unless supported by an appropriate research design.
- Present limitations alongside findings when necessary.

## 7. Intended Use

This project is an academic demonstration of data engineering, analytical methods, and data visualization.

Its findings are intended for educational and exploratory purposes. They should not be used as the basis for individual healthcare, insurance, or financial decisions.

The project demonstrates how structured healthcare data can be used to investigate questions about medication costs and insurance coverage while recognizing the importance of data quality, context, and responsible interpretation.
